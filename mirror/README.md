# Mirror: scroll-driven project page

A product-page-style site where scrolling plays the talk's animations, in the
talk's order. Each chapter pins one animation from the DeckWerk deck `grant_servicing/2610_TRI_Mirror_section` and plays it like a video once scrolling brings it into focus.

## Preview locally

```
python3 mirror/tools/serve.py 8000      # from the repo root, or with any path
open http://localhost:8000/mirror/
```

Use this server, not `python3 -m http.server`. The scrubbed clips seek constantly, and Chrome can only seek a video
when the server answers HTTP Range requests. `http.server` doesn't, so the clips would keep jumping back to their
first frame. GitHub Pages supports ranges.

## How it works

**Hero.** The video seen through the letters of "Mirror" is a 23 s loop of the agent in seven worlds, opening on
diamonds in survival: the BASALT waterfall, a row of DeepMind Control, Atari and OGBench clips, the lava escape,
FindCave, OGBench manipulation, and a tree in survival. A soft edge sweeps left to right between them, so the letters change one at a time.
Every clip is the agent's own execution, never a video plan or a human demo. Rows of clips are split at gaps between
letters (measured in Chromium); css sizes the word to .945 of the video's width at every size (`.hero-word`), so
each seam's dark gutter stays between two letters. `python3 mirror/tools/build_hero.py` rebuilds it; the edit
is the `SHOTS` list in that file, and `--sheet` previews it without encoding.

**Chapters.** A `<section class="chapter" data-scene="…" data-segs="…">` is a tall track with a sticky,
viewport-sized stage. Scrolling moves from chapter to chapter and only drives the focus changes: the headline rises
in, holds, and lifts away as the stage comes forward, and the stage dissolves as it scrolls on. A reader who stops
partway between the headline and the stage is carried on to one of them, the way they were scrolling, or to the stage
within 1.5 s of using the animation (`USED`: a swipe's first pixels can move the page either way, and one that had moved
it up was carried back to the headline, as if taken for a scroll). The scroll position no longer scrubs the animation.

Once the stage is in focus, the chapter plays like a video. Each click of the talk runs at normal speed, videos
included. Its last frame then holds until its text has had time to be read (the step in the list, or the scene's
title, at `READ_WPS` (4) words per second from when it showed, and at least the chapter's `data-read` value; nothing caps it, since the reader can swipe on), and the next click follows. The last click
holds, and the chapter has ended. A thin bar along the bottom of each animation's box (`.pbar`) shows how far it has
played: one segment per click, which fills over the click and its reading hold. A tap or click on the box pauses or plays it at once
(no selecting first); the first tap also shows the controls for about a second and a half: a pause or play glyph, a
chevron at each side and a line on the gestures. A swipe left goes on to the next click and a swipe right back to the
previous one, as iOS pages (the user flipped the earlier choice on 2026-10-15). On a desktop the arrow keys, a sideways
trackpad swipe and a mouse drag step too, and the box can be focused with Tab (Enter or Space pause). The arrow keys go
to the box in view wherever the focus is, except in a field, or in the gallery or the survival tabs while those are on
the screen: Chrome leaves the focus on a button after a click, so a gallery button or a tab clicked earlier used to
swallow the arrows until the reader clicked an animation.
Every box also has a transport (`.ctl-bar`): previous, pause or play (play again once ended), and next, as buttons
that work at once, and beside them a note, "or press ← →" (or "or swipe" on a touch screen), until the reader has used
the controls (`html.ctl-used`). The user asked on 2026-10-15 that nothing need a click first and that the controls be
visible. Where it sits depends on the room. On a wide screen it floats over the bottom of the animation (a scene's
transport is in its `.stage`, beside the frame, which clips; a figure holds its own) and shows as the chapter comes into
focus, while the pointer moves over the box and after a key (`cue`); a touch screen keeps it shown. It sits under the
box and stays there on a narrow screen (`fitCard` keeps `CTL_ROOM` for it, so the two tallest figures on an iPhone Pro
are drawn a few per cent narrower), on a wide one with room under the frame (`html.ctl-below`, set in `relayout`: an
iPad held sideways, a tall window), and under the figures beside their copy, which always have room. In a short window
(`html.short`) it stands beside the box as a column (`fitShort` keeps `CTL_SIDE` at either side and sets `--ctl-x`/`--ctl-y`).
The first chapter's old "Click to pause · arrow keys to step" hint is gone; the transport's note replaces it. Fingers go
through touch events: the first 8 px of a move decide its direction (sideways: 1.5 times as far as up or down, also for
the first touchmove, which Safari decides by), and a sideways one cancels its touchmoves so the
page can't scroll under it (`touch-action: pan-y` alone let slightly diagonal swipes scroll); a vertical swipe that
starts on the box scrolls the page. The box (`.ctl-box.selected`) lets go when it leaves the screen, when its animation
ends, or when another box is
selected, and a pause the reader made goes with it. The code is the "controls" block of `js/site.js`. A chapter pauses
by itself when it leaves focus and picks up where it was when it comes back. One that has ended plays from the start
when the reader comes to it again from above. With reduced motion nothing plays by itself: each click shows its last
frame, and a swipe steps to the next one. A chapter
doesn't start until its scene has drawn its opening with its videos and fonts loaded: until then its poster, that
opening frame as a still, stands in, and the scene fades in over it on the same frame. A reload, or coming back from another page, returns to the same place
(`site.js` saves it in `sessionStorage` and scrolls back after laying the page out, rather than the browser's own
restoration, which lands before the chapters have their height).

A chapter's `data-lead` (seconds) skips a still opening, so the animation moves as soon as it is in focus: it
plays from there, and from there again on replay. The values (intro 0.6, Minecraft 0.5, BASALT 0.7)
leave about 0.3 s before the first change on screen; exploration has none, since its opening is a click of its own. A chapter's `data-speed` plays its animation faster than the
talk, videos included: 1.25 for the intro and exploration, 1 for replanning, 1.3 for in-context and 1.8 for the
other results chapters.
It can also change partway through, as "T:speed" from scene time T on: the intro plays its "Adding noise" step at 0.4,
exploration its early rollouts at 1.8, and the benchmarks their "All methods learn…" title at 1, so that a title that
changes partway through a click is on screen long enough to read. Exploration plays each of its seven
beats as its own click (the deck fuses them into two; `scenes/scene-adapter.js`), so the controls step through each title. The reading holds after each click don't scale with it.

The timing constants are next to the chapter constants in `js/site.js` (`HEAD`, `REST`, `DWELL` set the scroll
lengths).

**Timing by hand.** Each chapter's `data-dwell` gives each click's dwell, in the reader's seconds from the click's
start to the next click, exactly (no reading time on top; an empty entry keeps the reading rule). A dwell shorter than
its click starts the next click that far in, and the click's own videos and moves play on under it (`Chapter.retime` in
`js/site.js`, `SCENE.retime` in `scenes/scene-adapter.js`, which carries the click's running beats into the next one with
their own clocks); only scenes whose beats keep their own clocks can (intro, BASALT, text conditioning, benchmarks).
`data-beats` makes each beat a scene plays straight through within one click a click of its own (`?beats` for the scene;
`data-speed` and `data-lead` follow the moved times, `REMAP`). The values were set by hand on 2026-10-17 and then
stretched where a title got under 3.5 words a second. Re-time them with `?timing` (`mirror/?timing`, `js/timing.js`):
in Record each click plays, then holds until you tap the animation, press → or the panel's Next, and a tap while it plays
starts the next click over its end. Test plays your times, Site the page's; the table shows both. The times are kept in
localStorage and posted to `tools/serve.py`'s log (`/tmp/mirror_phone_log.jsonl`, `"type": "timing"`), and Copy gives
each chapter's `data-dwell` (with `data-beats` and its `data-segs` where split). Dwells are in the reader's seconds:
re-time a chapter after changing its `data-speed`. (Record also holds the opening until you tap; that wait is recorded
but not played back, and the page has no `data-wait`.)

`data-segs` lists each click's duration in seconds. The scene reports its own durations when it loads and the page
re-lays itself out if they differ, so these values only spare the page a jump.

**Scenes.** Every animation keeps its deck timeline (`STEPS`, `stateAt(k, tau)`, `apply`). The adapter folds the
deck's clicks, including fused clicks whose chained beats overlap, into one continuous timeline, in the same way
the deck's mp4 export hook did. Video cues play natively when the timeline moves forward at about real speed, and are
seeked frame by frame otherwise. Opened without `?scroll`, a scene still steps on click and arrow keys, as in the
deck. The clips are encoded at the size they are shown (`ENCODE` in `tools/build_scenes.py`, a keyframe a second):
the intro has a 544 px set (`*_d.mp4`) and a 432 px set for phones held upright (`*_m.mp4`, picked by
`scene-adapter.js` through `window.CLIP_PHONE`); BASALT uses the deck's own encodes as they are (`*_g.mp4`). In
scroll mode a scene fetches only what its opening shows: its images wait in `data-src` and its clips start with
`preload="none"` (`lazy_media` in `tools/build_scenes.py`), and `scene-adapter.js` asks for each as a frame shows it,
for the next click's clips once the frame on screen has what it needs, and for the rest of the images once the page
shows the scene (`SCENE.more`).

On narrow or portrait screens the page draws the scene's title itself (`html.narrow`), in the grey box right above
the animation, so it stays legible: the caption block and the frame join into one box. `fitCard` in `js/site.js` sizes
the box to the screen and gives the caption block room for the scene's longest title (`SCENE.captions()`), so the box
keeps its size from click to click and any room left over goes to the screen's edges. The caption fades with the
scene's title and note (`SCENE.caption()` reports their opacity), and when it changes height the grey above the title
and the text glide to the new height together (`caption()` in `js/site.js`).

**Phones held sideways** aren't laid out for: `js/turn.js` (loaded in `<head>` of the page and both viewers) covers
the page with a note asking the reader to turn the phone upright (`html.turn`, `.turn-note` in `css/site.css`). A phone
is a touch screen whose shorter side is under 600px; held sideways means a landscape screen orientation and a window
more than 1.3 times as wide as tall, so tablets, opened foldables and near-square cover screens (Z Flip) keep their
layouts. Under the note the page holds still: the playing videos pause (also in an open modal), the chapters stop on
their frame, nothing loads, and `relayout()` skips, so turning back finds every chapter as it was with no reload.

**Short windows.** A window under 500px tall and wider than tall that isn't a phone (a laptop at 200% zoom) gets
`html.short` instead of `narrow`. The nav
slides away while a chapter holds the screen (`html.stage-on`), so each chapter has the full height. The animations
use the narrow layout's box, with the title drawn by the page above the animation's 1920×880 body, and `fitShort` in
`js/site.js` makes the box as large as the height allows, leaving any spare width at the sides. The two figure chapters
put the current step beside one figure, with the other steps shown as a row of segments above it. The CSS is in the
"fix: short landscape" block of `css/site.css`.

**Phones held upright.** On a narrow screen that is taller than wide (`html.portrait`), `site.js` loads each scene
with `?scroll&portrait`, and the scene lays itself out on a tall stage, usually 1080 wide and about 1300–1500 tall (the Minecraft
chart is taller). The frame takes the stage's shape from `SCENE.ready`. Each layout is `scenes/portrait/<name>.js`, which
sets `window.PORTRAIT`; the scene's own script reads it at the hooks in `PORTRAIT_HOOKS` (`tools/build_scenes.py`),
and every hook keeps the deck's value when `PORTRAIT` is null, so the landscape scenes are unchanged. The layouts
keep the deck's content, beats and timing and rearrange it to read top to bottom, starting at the top of the stage
right under the caption. A layout can also set `PORTRAIT.FILL`, how much of the stage each click fills (stage px, or
`{ h, at, dur }` to time the change): the frame then shows only that much, growing or shrinking from the bottom with
the scroll (`fillFrame` in `js/site.js`). The maps, plots and tiles stack, the BASALT table is transposed, and in the
suites the plots carry their metric as a rotated axis label and the summary table pushes them out of the frame, as
the BASALT table does its gallery. The gallery's two-up videos play stacked versions,
plan above execution (`data-portrait-src`, built by `tools/build_gallery.py`), and so do the survival
interlude's two tabs (each button's `data-portrait-video`; each tab's `data-ar` gives the screen its shape before
the clip loads).
`scenes/dreamer.html` is edited in place rather than rebuilt (see below), so it carries its portrait hooks by hand: `PORTRAIT_HEAD`, the
stage size, `PORTRAIT.arrange()` and the wave order `PORTRAIT.lag()`. Add them to `PORTRAIT_HOOKS["dreamer"]` if it
is ever rebuilt.

**Closed-loop replanning.** This section is the paper figure `website_figures/single_attempt_narrow_v2.pdf`,
redrawn as inline SVG in the page's colours and fonts. It is not the old `replanning.mp4`. It is a chapter without
the headline phase (`data-inline data-head="none"`), so it pins as it arrives. Its steps play one after another like the
animations, a line on the active step shows how far that step has played, and clicking a step plays it. `tools/extract_replan.py` reads the trajectory, re-plan points, failed attempts, milestone and plan corridor
from the PDF and writes `js/replan-geometry.js`. It reuses the line ranges, event indices and plans through the
milestone from `website_figures/drift_animation/animate.py`. The step timing is at the bottom of `js/replan.js`.

**In-context learning.** This section is the paper figure `figures/lava_escape.pdf` (also
`website_figures/lava_escape.pdf`), redrawn in the page. The paper sources have no script for it. In that PDF every
line is a filled outline rather than a stroke. `tools/extract_lava.py` (with `tools/pdfpaths.py`, a small PDF
content-stream interpreter) recovers each line's exact centreline from the outline's segment pieces, and maps page
coordinates to data through the axes' grid lines. It writes `js/lava-data.js`:

- every trajectory, in blocks, with its colour and width;
- the deaths and escapes, each matched to the life it ends;
- the spawn star and the mean-displacement arrows;
- the four escape-rate curves at lives 1 to 30, with their bands and markers.

To verify the extraction, overlay it on a render of the PDF: centrelines through the strokes, markers on the ×s and
◆s, curves through the dashes. The trajectory colours step along a colormap in drawing order, but the bias does not
grow with that order, so the colours only tell lives apart. The page doesn't present them as time. Only the axes,
labels and frame are restyled; no path is reshaped. Markers that end no life are the paper's in-plot legend
symbols, and are left out.

## Updating a scene from the deck

```
python3 mirror/tools/build_scenes.py                 # all six
python3 mirror/tools/build_scenes.py --only basalt   # one
```

The sources are the deck mirror's `assets/web/*.html`. The intro comes from
`~/mirror_talk/intro_anim/deck/index_envx_dissolve.html`, the page behind the deck's six intro mp4s. To use a newer
version of an animation, point `SOURCES` at its file and rebuild. Base64 media becomes files in `scenes/media/`, and
videos get short keyframe intervals so they scrub well. If a scene's click timing changed, also update its
`data-segs` (`window.MIRROR.chapters[i].segs` in the console shows the live values). Then redraw its posters,
`python3 mirror/tools/build_posters.py --only basalt` (also after changing a portrait layout, a `data-lead` or the
theme).

Every build runs `tools/theme.py`, so the deck keeps the talk's look (cream, Charter, Avenir) and the site gets the
page's. Each colour literal becomes the colour of the role it plays, read from `css/theme.css`. Mirror's rollouts,
paths, bars and curves take one blue, supervised Mirror a sky blue, plans green and failures red, and the deck's
warm greys move onto the theme's neutrals at the same lightness. The fonts become Source Sans 3. Caption keywords
match the page's headlines: a key claim (classes `blue`, `violet`, `teal`) is set in the gold gradient, problems
stay red and plans green. The Minecraft results chart (`dreamer`) keeps its original colours, and only its ground,
ink and fonts change. A build stops with an error if a scene uses a colour the theme doesn't map, so add new deck
colours to `tools/theme.py`. `python3 mirror/tools/theme.py --check` dry-runs the pass on the built
scenes.

`scenes/dreamer.html` is ahead of its deck source: some of its "Mirror + Oracle VP" bars differ from
`dreamer_steps.2f958b3d.html`. On 2026-10-15 it was themed in place rather than rebuilt. Find its newer source
before rebuilding it.

To look at one scene on its own, open `tools/scene_harness.html?scene=basalt`. It loads the scene the way the page
does: `INFO` holds its clicks, and `R(T)` renders time `T`.

## The survival videos (`#survival`)

The two tabs are each the fastest episode to its tab's furthest milestone in the episode viewer: Online (Generative
VP, the self-improving V2A model) episode 3 (index 2; iron pickaxe at 6:34, no deaths; no Online episode gets a
diamond) and Oracle VP episode 52 (index 51; diamond at 17:15, one death at 5:56 after which the agent rebuilds
everything). `python3 mirror/tools/build_survival.py`
builds both from the sources named in its docstring: each clip is the plan beside the execution at 640x360 a pane
(`media/survival/*.mp4`, and `*_portrait.mp4` stacked), 1x around the first time the agent gets, crafts or places
each thing (read from `eval/data`) and up to 32x otherwise, with green flashes where the plan re-syncs (every 8
frames). The clips carry no text: the pane tags, the speed, the milestone notes, the goal, the game clock and the
milestone icons are the page's own (`js/survival.js`, from the generated `js/survival-data.js`), so they stay sharp.
Clicking a milestone icon jumps the video to 3 s before that milestone and plays it. The bar under the video carries the
same milestones as tiny item icons where the clip reaches each (`.sv-marks`, from `js/survival.js`), faded until
then; they jump there too.
The plan shows by default; "Hide plan" crops the screen to the execution (`.sv-film` keeps the clip's shape against
the execution's edge, and `shape()` in `js/site.js` glides the screen's size). `--sheet` previews the 1x windows.

## Adding a visualization

- **A new stepped animation:** follow the deck pages' conventions (a 1920×1080 `#stage`, `STEPS` of
  `[id, prop, to, at, dur, ease]` tweens, `stateAt`, `apply`, `.title` elements for the narration). Add an entry to
  `PAGES` in `scenes/scene-adapter.js` saying how its chained beats hand over. Then add a chapter section to
  `index.html`.
- **Static content:** copy a `.split` section (media + copy) or a gallery `.card`. Videos with `class="lazy"` and a
  `data-src` load and play only when on screen. The gallery's clips hold no text (`tools/build_gallery.py` builds them
  from the raw recordings); the card's `data-panes` names the labels the page draws over them. A gallery card shows `media/posters/<video name>.jpg`, the video's
  first frame, until the video has loaded; cut one for a new card (and its `data-portrait-src`) with
  `ffmpeg -i <video> -frames:v 1 -q:v 4 media/posters/<video name>.jpg`.

**Publishing.** GitHub Pages lets browsers keep a file for 10 minutes, so every CSS and JS file the page and the
viewers load carries one version tag (`?v=...` in `index.html`, `eval/index.html`, `basalt/index.html`): a reader whose
page refreshed never mixes it with old scripts. Bump it on every publish:
`sed -i -E 's/\?v=[0-9a-z]+"/?v=NEW"/g' mirror/index.html mirror/eval/index.html mirror/basalt/index.html`.

## Loading on a slow connection

Everything that downloads at once shares a slow connection, so the page loads in reading order, one thing at a time
(`schedule` in `js/site.js`):

- Each chapter gets its poster first (`scenes/posters/`, 3-43 KB), then its scene, and the next chapter waits until
  that scene has what its frame on the screen needs (`SCENE.pending`). What a scene fetches ahead doesn't count, and a
  stalled scene holds the others back 8 s at most. Scenes load within 2.5 screens, or as the first chapter below the
  screen; the posters further on follow in order. The lava figure's paths (`js/lava-data.js`, 200 KB) load with its
  chapter (`data-scripts`); its screenshot, which is its first step, is its poster (`data-src`), and its first step's
  text shows from the start.
- The poster lies over the scene's frame, and the scene loads under it fully drawn; the poster fades out once the scene
  has drawn its opening with its clips (`Chapter.seeking`), or after `FIRST_MAX` (3 s) of its stage on the screen. iOS
  Safari doesn't start a clip in a frame at opacity 0: while the scene faded in over its poster, every iPhone visit
  (`?debug` logs from 10-16) waited out the old 8 s limit on its first animation, however fast the connection.
- A scene's clips load one at a time, in the order its timeline shows them (`pump` in `scenes/scene-adapter.js`): the
  next starts once the one before it is in, or after 2 s, and a clip a frame shows starts at once. The intro's opening
  clip came in at 700 kbps in 0.7 s instead of 1.5 s beside its click's two larger ones. On iOS a clip that hasn't
  played stays at readyState 1 with nothing buffered however much it has downloaded, so "in" also reads the clip's
  resource timing entries (`clipIn`); before that, each counted as on its way for 6 s and held the next chapter back.
- Downloads the reader has scrolled past give way to the rest: a scene's clips still on their way (`SCENE.park`), and
  the hero's montage or the survival clip once well off the screen (`park`). They go on in the background once
  everything the reader is on and coming to is in, one at a time and nearest first, and give way again whenever
  something else needs the line (`background`); the browser keeps what they had. A download the browser has only
  deferred counts as under way (Chrome reports it idle), and so do a gallery card's once it leaves the screen and every one
  still going as an episode modal opens: on GitHub Pages the page's files share one HTTP/2 connection, and five deferred
  video downloads (the hero's, the survival clip's, three of the gallery's) used up its flow-control window, so the
  modal's page got its headers and no body for 10 s and more (8 of 8 opens after the gallery, 10-17; none since). While the hero's montage or the
  survival clip plays on the screen, it gets 6 s buffered before anything else loads.
- The survival clips need 1.7-2.5 Mbps, more than slow 4G has (it stalled every second or two there). Each has a light
  copy, `media/survival/*_light.mp4` (20 fps at 896 x 252, or 448 x 504 stacked; ~0.3 Mbps, peaks capped at 0.6). The
  page plays it from the start where the browser reports Save-Data or a 2g/3g connection (as for `hero_small.mp4`),
  and switches to it, at the same moment, once the clip stalls 2.5 s (4 s just after a seek or a new clip) or three
  times in 20 s (`stallWatch` in `js/site.js`); it then keeps the light copies for the visit. Remake them from the
  full clips with ffmpeg: `-vf "fps=20,scale=896:252:flags=lanczos"` (`448:504` for `_portrait`), `-c:v libx264
  -preset slow -crf 30 -maxrate 600k -bufsize 1200k -g 40 -movflags +faststart -an`.
- `python3 mirror/tools/build_posters.py` redraws the posters with Playwright: each scene at its chapter's `data-lead`,
  as the deck's 16:9 frame with its title, as its 1920 x 880 body without one (narrow screens, short windows),
  and as its portrait stage cut to what the box shows at the start. `posters.js` also holds the scene's titles and its
  portrait stage's size, so the box has its final shape and caption before the scene arrives.

## Low Power Mode

iOS Low Power Mode (and iOS's mitigation for a hot phone; macOS on battery likewise) refuses every video a page plays
by itself (`NotAllowedError`) until the reader taps, and every animation's clips were empty boxes. Where WebCodecs
decodes video (iOS Safari 16.4 on), a refused video is drawn instead, and nothing waits for a tap:

- a scene draws its clips on a canvas in each slot (`startDrawing` in `scenes/scene-adapter.js`), its clip elements kept,
  paused and hidden, for their time (intro's action bars and BASALT's overlays read it);
- the page draws the hero's montage and the survival clip (`drawVideo` in `js/site.js`): `SceneDraw.takeOver` lays a canvas
  over the `<video>` and runs the element's own `play()`, `pause()`, `currentTime` and media events on a clock of its own,
  so the survival labels, milestones, bar, tabs and tap to pause work on unchanged. The survival clip plays its light
  copy then.

`scenes/scene-draw.js` (loaded on the first refusal) reads each MP4 by 512 KB ranges, decodes it with a `VideoDecoder` as
the clock moves, and holds a few frames at a time; a jump starts again from the keyframe before it (about one a second in
these clips). Without WebCodecs, an animation with clips waits on its poster with the play glyph, and the reader's tap
lets every video on the page and in the scenes loaded so far play (`unlockVideos`). Test it in Chromium by refusing
`play()` outside a tap (the harness's init script); `?debug` logs `refused`, `unlocked` and the drawn clips (`drawn`).

## Fonts

Everything is set in Source Sans 3 (SIL Open Font License), self-hosted in `fonts/` as variable-weight woff2
files for the latin, latin-ext and greek subsets. `css/theme.css` loads them for the page and `scenes/scene-fonts.css`
for the animations. The hero word is ExtraBold with no tracking, which leaves a gap between "r" and "o" for the
montage's tile seam (`GAP` in `tools/build_hero.py`). Re-measure the gaps and rebuild the hero clip if the face or
weight changes.

## The episode viewer (`eval/`)

`eval/index.html` shows the 384 evaluation episodes of the Diamond Challenge figure in the look of the survival
section: three tabs (Online · generated plans, Online · oracle plans, Offline · generated plans; Online opens), a line on
what that setting was trained on, and a grid of every episode's still, furthest first, each marked with the icon of the
furthest of the paper's twelve milestones it reached. An episode plays in the survival section's player, live on the
streamed episode (`js/watch-player.js`): the execution, with the video plan beside it (above it on a phone held
upright; hidden until "Show plan", which then stays for the next episodes), the survival tags with the speed, a note as
each milestone is first reached or the agent dies, a scrub line with each milestone as a tiny item icon (icons too close
for one each share one place, showing the furthest), the goal and the game clock, the twelve milestones (each jumps
there; `[` and `]` step), and a caption: the outcome and the deaths, then the setting's line. A pill on the video starts
at 1× and each tap makes it faster: 2×, 4×, 8×, 16×, then Auto (16× between subgoal changes, 1× around each), and 1×
again. The headline is "Episode 101 got as far as the wooden pickaxe" where it fits on one line; on a phone the
episode's number moves to the eyebrow over "Got a wooden pickaxe" (both are in the page; css shows one). The
plan is cut from the episode's `plan_idm` video, its right half below the label row (`plan_box` in
`tools/build_survival.py`); the survival clips' green re-sync flashes are not drawn, as their timing is unverified for
the other settings.

- It uses `css/theme.css`, `css/site.css` (the survival section's classes) and `css/watch.css`; `eval/watch.js` is its
  own. Its asset links carry `?v=`: bump it with a change, or a phone may keep the old copy.
- The stills are `eval/thumbs/<tab>/<nn>.webp`, from `tools/build_thumbs.py` (the diamond if found, else the furthest
  block mined; a dark still is brightened, the videos are not).
- The videos are not in this repository: they stream from the Hugging Face dataset
  [hwryu/mirror_diamond_eval](https://huggingface.co/datasets/hwryu/mirror_diamond_eval) (HTTP Range, so seeking
  works).
- `eval/data/` (the per-episode events) and `eval/icons/` (the paper's milestone icons) are generated; don't edit them by
  hand. A deep link opens an episode at a time: `eval/#online/8@600` is Online (Generative VP) episode 8 at 10:00.
- The page opens it as a modal from the survival section ("Watch all 384 evaluation episodes").

## The BASALT episode viewer (`basalt/`)

`basalt/index.html` shows the 44 BASALT evaluation episodes: FindCave and MakeWaterfall, each with the supervised V2A
model (the video planner with VPT's pretrained inverse dynamics model) and the self-improving one (the video planner
with the V2A model trained by self-play), on the 11 worlds of the BASALT 2022 challenge. Each task tab has a line on the
task and a grid per model (never mixed), each still marked ✓ or ✗. An episode plays in the same player, with a switch
to the other model on the same world. FindCave episodes carry the judged time of the success (the cave entry); a
MakeWaterfall episode ends where the agent ends it (its `end` in `data/index.json`: its video plan turning gray, its
"picture", or the time limit), and success is judged there. Both were checked by eye for all 28 successes on
2026-10-16: the cave entries are right, and the MakeWaterfall ends come after the waterfall is built, never at its
placing (the data has no time for that). The verdict is a gold dot on the scrub line and a note on the video, half a
second early at an end (a self-improving episode's end is its video's last frame, which playback never quite reaches);
"Jump to the success" (or "Jump to the end" of a failed early end) lands just before it. Only MakeWaterfall has video
plans: the dataset's FindCave folders hold the recordings alone.

- Like `eval/`, it uses `css/watch.css` and `js/watch-player.js` (its own script is `basalt/watch.js`) and streams its
  videos from [hwryu/mirror_diamond_eval](https://huggingface.co/datasets/hwryu/mirror_diamond_eval) (folders
  `basalt_*`). Its `data/` is generated; `icons/steve.png` is not used.
- The page opens it as a modal from the BASALT chapter's headline ("Watch all 44 evaluation episodes"). A deep link:
  `basalt/#waterfall` (a task) or `basalt/#waterfall/self_improving/3@60` (an episode).

## The episode modals (`js/modal.js`)

The viewers aren't linked from the hero: each opens from the results it belongs to, as a modal in the style of
Apple's product pages. The page dims and blurs, a dark sheet rises from below, and its round close button, Esc, a
click on the dimmed page or the browser's Back closes it, with the page where it was. A link with
`data-modal="diamond-episodes"` or `data-modal="basalt-episodes"` (and optionally `data-modal-hash`, a tab or episode in
the viewer's own `#hash` form) opens one; its `href` is the viewer's own page, for a new tab or without JavaScript.

- The sheet holds the viewer in a frame with `?embed` (`js/embed.js`): its nav and footer go, and an episode opens as
  a view pushed over the list, with "All episodes" leading back, instead of a window over the page. The two talk by
  `postMessage` (the messages are listed in `js/embed.js`). Each viewer loads once and stays loaded; the page's videos
  pause while a modal is open.
- While open, the URL is `#diamond-episodes` or `#basalt-episodes`, followed by the viewer's hash
  (`#diamond-episodes/online/8`), so a link opens the modal at that episode, over the page at the modal's section.
  `js/site.js` doesn't take these hashes as anchor jumps.
